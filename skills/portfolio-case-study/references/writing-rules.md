# Writing rules — defaults when house rules are silent

House rules always win. These cover gaps.

## The story every case study needs

1. **Who and what was wrong.** Client (or self-initiated), and the actual
   problem — not the deliverable. "They had five products and no identity
   holding them together", not "The brief was to create a brand identity."
2. **Constraint.** What made it hard: budget, scale, legacy, audience, size.
3. **Decisions with reasons.** "I did X because Y." A decision without a reason
   is a deliverables list.
4. **A rejected direction.** What you tried and dropped, and why. This is the
   single biggest signal of real design thinking.
5. **What shipped.** Concrete. What it holds up against.
6. **Evidence, if you have it.** Only real numbers/quotes from the notes.

Short case studies keep 1, 3, 5 and fold the rest in.

## Turning a raw transcript into copy

- Pull facts first, write second. Make the fact list from step 4 of BUILD.
- Keep the user's own phrases when they are good — that is their voice. Cut the
  filler around them ("so basically", "kind of", "and then we like").
- Spoken notes over-explain early and rush the ending. Rebalance: less setup,
  more decisions.
- If the user says something specific and vivid, it almost certainly belongs in
  the case study.

## Sentence level

- Active voice. Someone did something.
- Concrete nouns. "A mark that survives on a 6mm switch plate" beats "a
  versatile and scalable logo".
- Cut adjectives that fit any project: cohesive, seamless, robust, holistic,
  innovative, impactful, effective, user-centric.
- No process-speak without an object: "ideated", "iterated", "explored" —
  iterated on *what*, into *what*?
- Don't narrate the images ("as seen above"). Captions say what the image
  can't.

## Captions (if the format uses them)

- One line. What it is + why it matters, or nothing.
- Consistent punctuation — pick one and record it in house rules.

## Alt text

Always write it, even if the platform hides it. Describe what is in the image
literally; one sentence.
